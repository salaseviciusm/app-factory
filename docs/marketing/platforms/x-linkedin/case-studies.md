# Build-in-Public Case Studies — Successes, Failures, and Where They Are Now

**Research date:** August 2026. Companion to `algorithm.md` and `audience.md`.

Eight cases below: six bootstrapped/indie, plus one bootstrapped consumer-app counterexample and one solo-creator case included specifically because it proves the audience-mismatch point rather than refuting it. Four of the eight are failures or partial failures. Every number has a source and a date; where a widely-repeated number has no traceable source, it is flagged.

**The pattern to watch for as you read:** in every profitable case here, the product is sold to *the same kind of person who reads the posts*. There is not one case in this file of a build-in-public account driving meaningful adoption of a **consumer** app.

---

## 1. Pieter Levels / Photo AI — the biggest indie build-in-public success, and the caveat inside it

**Who:** Pieter Levels (@levelsio), solo, no employees, no funding. ~500K+ X followers.
**What:** Nomad List (now Nomads.com), Remote OK, Photo AI, Interior AI.

**What he posts.** Raw revenue screenshots, code screenshots, MRR milestones, failure counts, single-file PHP flexes. Format: short post + one screenshot. No threads, no "5 lessons," no CTA most of the time.

**Real posts:**
- $18,000 MRR / 587 customers announcement — https://x.com/levelsio/status/1636408627371323393
- $420,000/month record with per-product breakdown — https://x.com/levelsio/status/1837707857372106992
- "40,870 line index.php making $105,000/mo revenue and $80,000/mo profit" — https://levels.io/photoai-40870-line-index-php-105k-mo-revenue

**Numbers.** Photo AI launched 10 Feb 2023 → ~$5.4K MRR week 1 → $28.7K MRR by April 2023 → $61.8K by July 2023 → $100K+ by Sept 2024 → **$132–138K MRR as of Nov 2025**, ~70% of his total income, ~$1.6M ARR (https://www.indiehackers.com/post/photo-ai-by-pieter-levels-complete-deep-dive-case-study-0-to-132k-mrr-in-18-months-3a9a2b1579).

**CTA:** essentially none. The product link is in his bio and in the screenshots. He does not ask.

### ⚠️ The caveat that matters most to us

In the $18K MRR post, Levels writes: **"Added $7,000 in MRR in a day from one TikTok!"** — https://x.com/levelsio/status/1636408627371323393

The single largest same-day revenue jump in the most famous build-in-public account on X came from **TikTok**, not from X. He has also credited the Lex Fridman podcast as a major growth catalyst. His X audience is his *reputation surface*; his customers came from elsewhere.

**Where is he now (Aug 2026).** Still running everything, still profitable. But he has publicly acknowledged the decline of his originals: Remote OK peaked ~6 years after launch, Nomads peaked ~8 years after launch, and he described the digital-nomad market that "exploded in 2014" as having largely "died by 2025" — https://x.com/levelsio/status/2072079405287960857. Nomad List did >$1.5M ARR at its peak; it lost >50% of revenue overnight during COVID. **Even the archetype's flagship products have a lifecycle, and the audience did not save them.**

## 2. Tony Dinh / TypingMind — the cleanest "audience → launch → revenue" case on X

**Who:** Tony Dinh (@tdinh_me), solo, Vietnam. Grew **100 → 181,000+ X followers**.
**What:** DevUtils, Xnapper, Black Magic, TypingMind.

**What he posts.** Dev-tool progress, revenue milestones, and — crucially — failure stories. Short posts with screenshots and demo GIFs.
**Profile:** https://x.com/tdinh_me

**Numbers.**
- TypingMind hit **$10K MRR within 7 days of launch** ($22K in licence revenue in week one) — https://startupfounderstories.com/stories/tony-dinh-typingmind-22k-7-days
- $45K MRR Sept 2024 → **$145K MRR Oct 2025**, with the B2B "Teams" tier now >50% of revenue
- Crossed **$1M in total earnings in 2025**, reportedly working ~20 hrs/week
- Running ~$137K MRR in 2026 — https://supabird.io/articles/from-100-to-180k-followers-tony-dinh-s-twitter-x-growth-tactics-that-still-work-in-2026

**CTA:** launch posts with a direct product link; otherwise product mentions embedded in progress updates.

**Where is he now (Aug 2026).** Still shipping, TypingMind is the main business and has drifted from prosumer to B2B teams — which is the tell. The audience got him to $10K MRR in a week; **the growth from $45K to $145K came from selling to companies, not from tweets.**

**Why this case is real and not survivorship noise:** the launch-day number is genuinely attributable to the audience. If you have 100K+ dev followers and you launch a dev tool, X is a legitimate launch channel worth five figures on day one. That is a narrow, real, category-specific claim — and it does not transfer to a calorie tracker.

## 3. Marc Lou — $1M/year from build-in-public, and the honest accounting inside it

**Who:** Marc Lou (@marc_louvion), solo, ~100K+ X followers, 17 products shipped.
**What:** ShipFast, CodeFast, DataFast, TrustMRR, and a graveyard.

**Primary source (his own numbers, published):** https://newsletter.marclou.com/p/i-made-1-032-000-in-2025

**Numbers, 2025.**
- **Total: $1,032,000 — down 20% from 2024**
- ShipFast ~$20K/mo, CodeFast ~$20K/mo, DataFast $15.8K/mo (~1,000 paying customers, 7% churn, 14% MoM growth), TrustMRR ~$200K+ for the year
- Investing returns $147K
- **X (creator payouts): $14,339 for the entire year — while gaining 100,000 new followers**
- Jan 2026: $94,799/month, with TrustMRR now #1 at $31.4K — https://indieai.directory/blog/marc-lou-81683-february-2026-income-breakdown/

**What he posts.** Revenue screenshots, launch-day threads, "I built X in 24 hours" posts, opinionated takes on shipping fast. High-frequency, low-polish, personality-forward.

**CTA:** direct — product links in launch posts, affiliate/referral loops between his own products.

### The three things this case actually proves

1. **X payouts are not a business.** $14,339 for 100K new followers. Anyone modelling creator revenue as a channel should use that number.
2. **Every product is sold to founders.** ShipFast (boilerplate for founders), CodeFast (course for founders), DataFast (analytics for founders), TrustMRR (revenue verification for founders). His audience *is* his market. This is why it works — and it is the exact condition a consumer app studio cannot satisfy.
3. **Two 2025 launches failed anyway.** BioAge and ClipMarc both failed despite full access to his ~500K-reach audience. His own conclusion, verbatim: **"having an audience doesn't guarantee a successful startup."**

**Where is he now (Aug 2026).** Bigger than ever in revenue terms, but the composition shifted: ShipFast — the product that made him famous — has fallen from #1 to roughly 4th, and revenue is 20% off its peak. The boilerplate-for-founders market got saturated by his own imitators.

## 4. Ruben Hassid / EasyGen — the strongest LinkedIn indie case, and a perfect mirror of the same trap

**Who:** Ruben Hassid, bootstrapped, no VC. Grew **8,000 → 400,000+ LinkedIn followers in two years**; ~480K by early 2025.
**What:** EasyGen — an AI tool for writing LinkedIn posts.

**Real posts:**
- "EasyGen grew my LinkedIn to 480k followers. Then my 2 employees' accounts to 25k & 30k." — https://x.com/RubenHssd/status/1885715482684293310
- "EasyGen works because I don't care about growing to exit it. It's bootstrapped." — https://x.com/RubenHssd/status/1870854532067979647

**Format:** short-line LinkedIn text posts and carousels, personal-narrative openers, heavy first-line hooks. Exactly the format `algorithm.md` §2.1/§2.5 predicts wins (dwell + comments, no outbound links).

**Numbers.** >$9,100 MRR within 92 days of launch → **$41,500 MRR by Nov 2024** → **~$540,000 ARR**, 26,000+ weekly users, ~18% of revenue via affiliates, 100% organic (no paid ads) — https://www.starterstory.com/easy-gen-breakdown, https://www.rewardful.com/articles/how-easygen-built-revenue-stream-through-affiliate-marketing

**CTA:** product link in featured section / comments, plus a free-trial offer; the posts themselves are value-first with the tool as proof.

**Where is he now (Aug 2026).** Still running EasyGen, still bootstrapped, still posting. He has explicitly said he isn't building to exit.

**The mirror.** EasyGen is a LinkedIn tool sold to LinkedIn creators, marketed on LinkedIn, whose primary proof point is the founder's own LinkedIn follower count. It is the most self-consistent go-to-market in this file — and completely unreproducible for anything that isn't a LinkedIn tool. **Note also the meta-problem:** EasyGen is one of the engines behind LinkedIn's AI-content flood (see `credibility.md`), and LinkedIn shipped a "Seems Like AI Slop" reporting control in July 2026. This business's core channel is now actively being cleaned up by the platform it depends on.

## 5. Tibo Louis-Lucas / Tweet Hunter + Taplio — the biggest exit, and what it actually paid

**Who:** Thibault "Tibo" Louis-Lucas (@tibo_maker). Previously bankrupt.
**What:** Tweet Hunter (X growth tool) and Taplio (LinkedIn growth tool). Sold to lempire (lemlist).

**Numbers.** Headline price **$10M**. Actual structure: **$2M upfront + $8M earn-out** tied to scaling from $1.5M ARR to $10M ARR. They reached $8M ARR, collecting $6M of the earn-out. Total **$8M**; after 18 months of grinding and French taxes, **just under $3M net** — https://joinhampton.com/blog/thibault-tibo-sold-8m-regrets-indie-hacker-portfolio, https://theygotacquired.com/saas/pony-express-acquired-by-lempire/

**Growth method — and this is the uncomfortable bit.** He gave influencers **25–30% of the equity** in Tweet Hunter and Taplio to drive engagement. The "build in public" surface was, materially, a paid-influence distribution deal wearing a founder's face.

**Where is he now (Aug 2026).** Sold, publicly says he regrets the sale, building again. Tweet Hunter and Taplio continue under lempire.

**What we take from it.** (a) Announced exit numbers are gross, structured, and pre-tax — the real number was ~30% of the headline. (b) The two biggest social-media growth tools were both built by giving away a quarter of the company to people with audiences. If a build-in-public success story looks organic, check whether someone was paid in equity.

## 6. FAILURE — Roman Koch: six apps, 2,461 downloads, $1,464 for the year

**Who:** Roman Koch, solo indie developer, active on X, LinkedIn, and YouTube.
**What:** six shipped apps in 2025.

**Source:** his own 2025 recap — https://medium.com/@romankoch/my-2025-recap-as-an-indie-developer-6846593eaad6

| App | Downloads | Revenue |
|---|---:|---:|
| CookieTime | ~78 | $0 |
| Split & Tip | ~50 | $0 |
| Blood Pressure App | 334 | ~$30 |
| SmokeFree | ~1,480 | ~$700 |
| EasyPost | 61 | €13 |
| ThinkPool | 458 | free |
| **Total** | **~2,461** | **$1,464** |

**What worked:** App Store Optimization. His words — after improving icons and screenshots, "optimization actually worked."
**What didn't:** the social channels. He lists X, LinkedIn and YouTube as active channels and reports **no attributable results from any of them.** His own summary: *"Marketing beats code. Every time."*

**Why this is the most relevant case in the file.** This is a consumer mobile app portfolio — the exact shape of our studio — run by someone who did post publicly on both platforms for a year. The output was 2,461 downloads and $1,464. **This is the realistic base rate for consumer apps marketed on X and LinkedIn.** Everything above it in this document is a dev tool or a founder tool.

**Where is he now.** Still shipping, now prioritising ASO.

## 7. FAILURE — Marc Lou's BioAge and ClipMarc: maximum audience, zero product-market fit

Pulled out separately from §3 because it is the cleanest controlled experiment available.

**The setup:** a founder with ~500K X followers, 100K of them added that same year, a proven launch playbook, and an email list — launching two new products in 2025.
**The result:** **both failed.** BioAge and ClipMarc are listed in his own annual review under failed launches, contributing to a residual "~$20K other" line.
**His conclusion, verbatim:** *"having an audience doesn't guarantee a successful startup."*
**Source:** https://newsletter.marclou.com/p/i-made-1-032-000-in-2025

**Diagnosis.** Both were outside the founder-tools category his audience buys. The audience showed up — the *market* didn't exist in it. Reach was never the constraint.

## 8. FAILURE — Anthony Castrio / Indie Worldwide: the build-in-public community that lost money

**Who:** Anthony Castrio, founder of Indie Worldwide, a paid community for bootstrapped founders. A build-in-public advocate running a build-in-public business.

**Numbers, published by him:**
- Trailing 12-month revenue: **$37,980**
- Trailing 12-month expenses: **$51,560**
- **Net: −$13,580**
- Paid himself $4,000/mo from a business that rarely profited that much; spent $1,000+/mo on tools
- Ran out of runway and **moved back in with his parents**

**Source:** https://anthonycastrio.substack.com/p/i-made-a-mistake

**What happened:** raised prices $29 → $49, removed the monthly plan, accidentally disabled billing (losing $1,000+), abandoned his best marketing channel to run time-consuming events. Growth stalled for nearly a year.

**Where is he now.** He reversed every decision — restored $29/mo, restored monthly plans, cut costs, resumed daily community standups — and recovered.

**Why it's in here:** the person selling community to indie founders, doing everything the movement recommends, in the most sympathetic possible audience, lost $13,580 in a year. Publishing your numbers is not a business model, and having the ideal audience is not the same as having a viable one.

## 9. FAILURE CLUSTER — "engagement was real, revenue was zero"

From a well-commented Indie Hackers thread on exactly this failure mode — https://www.indiehackers.com/post/your-build-in-public-audience-is-not-your-market-i-learned-the-difference-the-slow-way-2cbea1089d

| Product | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Flowly** (freelancer tool) | Months optimising build-in-public writing; a post hit 500 views / 50 comments in 24h. Commenters were founders and builders, not freelancers. Actual customers came from freelancer communities and "the searches a freelancer types at 11pm when an invoice is overdue." |
| **LeaseBase** (AI property mgmt) | Milestone posts on IH: *"Zero signups from any of it."* Real traction came from BiggerPockets and r/landlord. One thread about security-deposit deadlines outperformed **every** build-in-public post combined. |
| **Money Me** (personal finance) | Months of posting on IH and X with solid engagement. *"Zero paying customers from it."* Support threads where users complained about existing tools converted at **5–10×** the rate. |
| **ShellYard** (infra tooling) | Target buyers are sysadmins "actively allergic to marketing" who have never encountered build-in-public content. |

**The diagnosis, stated plainly:** *"Engagement is real. The revenue implication is roughly zero."*

## 10. COUNTEREXAMPLE — Cal AI: the bootstrapped consumer app that did $30M+ and never built in public

Included because it is the closest real-world analogue to what our studio makes.

**Who:** Zach Yadegari, teenager, bootstrapped, no outside funding.
**What:** Cal AI — photo-based calorie tracking. A consumer mobile app.

**Numbers.** Launched May 2024 → first $1M in sales by Sept 2024 → **>$30M revenue in 2025** → reportedly **$5.7M in monthly revenue by Jan 2026**, ~$40M ARR, 20–30K downloads/day. Sold to MyFitnessPal in 2026.
Sources: https://www.cnbc.com/2025/09/06/cal-ai-how-a-teenage-ceo-built-a-fast-growing-calorie-tracking-app.html, https://www.forbes.com/sites/zoyahasan/2026/03/06/this-u30-kept-launching-apps-until-one-worked-then-sold-it-to-myfitnesspal/, https://getlatka.com/companies/calai.app

**Growth engine:** a paid network of **~250 TikTok and Instagram creators on monthly retainers**, posting native-style videos. Not tweets. Not carousels. Not MRR screenshots.

**The comparison that should settle the argument.** Same year, same bootstrapped constraint, same consumer mobile category:

| | Channel | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Roman Koch (§6) | X + LinkedIn + YouTube, build-in-public | $1,464 |
| Cal AI | ~250 paid TikTok/IG creators | $30M+ |

Four orders of magnitude, and the difference is the channel.

## 12. The audience-mismatch failure mode, stated explicitly

This is the thing to internalise.

**The mechanism.** Build-in-public content is *about building*. The only people who reliably find content about building interesting are people who build. So the audience you accumulate is, by construction, filtered to founders and makers. That filter is applied automatically and invisibly, regardless of what your product does.

**Why it feels like it's working.** Founders are the most generous engagers on the internet. They like, they reply, they quote, they wish you well. Under both platforms' ranking systems (`algorithm.md`), replies and comments are the highest-weighted signals — so a builder audience mechanically produces *more reach*, which produces more builder followers, which produces more engagement. The loop is self-reinforcing and emotionally rewarding, and its conversion rate to a consumer product is approximately zero.

**Why it's worse than doing nothing.** It isn't neutral. It costs the hours you would have spent on channels that contain buyers, and it produces a false positive signal that tells you the product is working when the *post* was working. Marc Lou had 500K followers and two dead launches. Roman Koch had three social channels and $1,464.

**The three tests that break the illusion:**
1. **Name test.** Can you name the job, hobby, or situation of your buyer without using the word "founder," "indie," "maker," or "builder"? If not, your audience is not your market.
2. **Venue test.** Where does your buyer already complain about this problem, by name? BiggerPockets, r/landlord, a fishing forum, a nurses' Facebook group, the App Store review section of a competitor. If that venue is not X or LinkedIn, X and LinkedIn are not your channel.
3. **Attribution test.** For the last 10 paying customers, what did they see first? If the honest answer is "I don't know" or "a tweet, I think," you don't have a channel — you have a habit.

**When build-in-public genuinely works — the full and complete list:**
- Your product is for developers.
- Your product is for founders/makers.
- Your product is a social-media tool for the platform you're posting on.
- You are building the *studio's* reputation for hiring, partnerships, press, or a future B2B product — and you are honest that this is brand, not acquisition.

Everything else in this file is either one of those four, or a failure.
