# TikTok App Marketing — Case Studies

**Scope:** TikTok only. 6 wins (5 bootstrapped/indie, no VC at the point that mattered) + 5 failures/flameouts.

**Reading warning.** Indie app growth numbers are almost always **founder-reported and unaudited**. Starter Story, Indie Hackers, X threads and podcast interviews are the primary record; there is no equivalent of an S-1. Where a number is founder-claimed I say so. Where sources disagree I show the disagreement rather than picking the flattering one. Sensor Tower/Appfigures figures are **modelled estimates**, not actuals.

---

# WINS

## 1. Cal AI — bootstrapped, teen founders, TikTok-native product design

**What it is:** photo-based calorie tracker. Point phone at food, get macros.
**Funding:** **Bootstrapped, no VC.** Founders Zach Yadegari (17 at launch) and Henry Langmack; Blake Anderson also credited as a cofounder in earlier coverage.

**What they posted.** Two layers:
1. **Founder organic** — Yadegari recorded TikToks himself in the early months.
2. **Paid creator seeding at scale** — a network reported at ~250 creators on TikTok and Instagram on **monthly retainers**, the largest paid "tens of thousands of dollars per month for four posts," posting **native-style videos** (not ads) using the app. `[Founder-adjacent reporting via [Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/case-study/cal-ai) — treat the 250 figure as approximate]`

**The hook/format.** Point camera at a plate → number appears. That's the entire video. It works because the payoff *is* the demo, and it lands inside 3 seconds.

**The most transferable thing they did — and it's a product decision, not a marketing one.** Yadegari, in Forbes:
> "Making sure it had such a clean, simple, elegant design and feel, that it could be understood within three seconds of a TikTok."
> — [Forbes, 6 Mar 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/zoyahasan/2026/03/06/this-u30-kept-launching-apps-until-one-worked-then-sold-it-to-myfitnesspal/)

They designed the *first screen of the app* against the constraint of the *first three seconds of a TikTok*. Revealingly, only ~30% of logged calories actually came from the photo feature — most users logged by weight or barcode. **The camera feature was the marketing; the barcode scanner was the product.**

**Numbers** (all reported, dates attached):

| Metric | Value | Date | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Launch | — | May 2024 | Forbes |
| First $1M revenue | $1M | ~Sept 2024 (4 months) | [WhataStartup](https://whatastartup.substack.com/p/two-gen-z-founders-bootstrapped-cal-ai) |
| Downloads at 4 months | 100,000 | Sept 2024 | Starter Story |
| New downloads/day | 20,000–30,000 | May 2025 | Micro Empires |
| 2025 revenue | **$30M** | FY2025 | Forbes |
| Jan 2026 revenue | **$5.7M in the month** (~$50M annualised) | Jan 2026 | [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/ben-sherry/he-built-an-ai-app-in-high-school-made-40m-and-sold-to-myfitnesspal-now-hes-aiming-even-bigger/91307748) |
| Total users | 15M+ | Mar 2026 | Forbes |
| Price | $30/year | — | Forbes |
| Ad spend at scale | **$1M+/month** against $5.7M revenue | Jan 2026 | Inc. |

**CTA:** app name on screen + "link in bio" + creators naming it in comments. No discount codes, no urgency mechanics.

**Why it worked:** (a) the demo *is* the product; (b) they treated organic as a creative-testing lab and only then scaled into paid; (c) a $30/year price is an impulse purchase; (d) creator content ran from **creator handles**, reading as recommendation not advertising.

**Where are they now:** **Acquired by MyFitnessPal, announced week of 6 March 2026.** Terms undisclosed. Yadegari and seven Cal AI staff joined MyFitnessPal. Full success, clean exit.

**Caveat for us:** the paid-creator retainer network is the part we cannot copy on zero budget. The parts we *can* copy — 3-second-legible product design, organic-as-testing-lab — are free.

## 2. Quittr — bootstrapped, one video did the heavy lifting

**What it is:** porn/compulsive-behaviour quitting app with streaks, gamification and community.
**Funding:** **Bootstrapped, no VC.** Alex Slater (19) and Connor McLaren, seeded with ~$3,000 of their own money.

**What they posted.** Paid creator seeding, deliberately targeting **small close-knit communities over big follower counts** ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/quittr-breakdown)). Creators had to **show the app on screen**, with captions they could tweak; deals included **usage rights so Quittr could re-run the clips as ads** — i.e. Spark Ads seeding built into the contract from day one.

**Creator economics** — the most concretely documented indie creator deal I found anywhere ([Startup Spells](https://startupspells.com/p/porn-addiction-app-quittr-250k-mrr-4-months)):
- Named creator **Jeremiah Jones** (~2M followers): **$3 per 1,000 views** for 1M views/month
- Alternative structure: **$4,000 for 1M guaranteed views** (~$250k views per video across 4 videos)
- Smaller tier: **$1,500 for 500,000 views**, prorated down if the creator underperformed
- Payment: **20% upfront, balance after Apple payout** — so the app's cash cycle funded the campaign

**The breakout.** One Jeremiah Jones video: **9.9M views → $40,000 in a day → $100,000 in 65 hours.**

**Numbers:**

| Metric | Value | Date | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| First month revenue | $3,000 → $37,000 | ~Nov 2024 | LA Weekly |
| MRR at 4 months | $250,000 | early 2025 | Startup Spells |
| Downloads at 6 months | 350,000 across 120 countries | 2025 | LA Weekly |
| Total revenue at 6 months | $1.1M | 2025 | LA Weekly |
| MRR / downloads at ~9 months | **~$500K MRR, 1M downloads** | 2025 | Founder-claimed via [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/alex_slater), [InfoMoney](https://x.com/infomoney/status/1961938239758950555) |
| Price | **$6.99/week or $29.99/year, no free trial** | — | Starter Story |

**Source disagreement, flagged:** Starter Story's Quittr page reports **$50K/month** and dates the snapshot around Dec 2024, while LA Weekly and later founder statements report $250K–$500K MRR through 2025. These are probably different points on the same curve, but the $50K figure and the $250K figure cannot both describe the same month. **Do not quote a single Quittr MRR number as fact.**

**CTA:** creator says the name, app is visible on screen, link in bio, comment section answers "what's it called."

**Why it worked:** shame-driven private purchase (no social friction to buying), streak mechanics that generate their own content ("day 90"), no free trial so every install is immediately monetised, and creator deals priced **per view** rather than per post — which caps downside on a dud.

**Where are they now:** still operating and growing as of 2026; founder publicly targeting $1–3M/month. Alive.

## 3. Blake Anderson (RizzGPT/Plug AI, Umax) — the $50-creator experiment

**What it is:** RizzGPT (AI dating-app reply generator, later rebranded Plug AI) and Umax (AI face-rating / "looksmaxxing").
**Funding:** **Bootstrapped, no VC.** Built in an attic, non-engineer, heavy ChatGPT use, ~1 month build.

**The single most instructive tactic in this whole document.** Anderson did **not** hire big influencers. He found **two relatively unknown, underrated TikTok creators in the dating-advice niche and paid them $50 each** to naturally mention the app in their content.

**Result:** both videos went viral overnight → millions of views → **200,000 downloads in a week → $80,000 in month one.** `[Founder-claimed, reported via [Medium/oswd_1](https://medium.com/@oswd_1/how-a-23-year-old-non-engineer-built-rizz-gpt-a-solo-app-that-earned-3-6m-c7debc819da5) and [Pickle Rooms](https://picklerooms.com/blogs/origin-stories/blake-anderson-building-rizzgpt-and-umax)]`

**Portfolio numbers** (founder-stated on X, Oct 2024 — [@blakeandersonw](https://x.com/blakeandersonw/status/1842349939088925146)):
- RizzGPT: **$2.4M ARR**
- Umax: **$5M ARR**
- Cal AI: **$8M ARR**
Later reporting: RizzGPT $3.6M, Umax $6.3M lifetime; Umax **~7M downloads at ~$3.99/week** ([Whop](https://whop.com/blog/looksmaxxing-blake-anderson/)). Forbes covered the portfolio at **$15M ARR bootstrapped** ([Forbes, Oct 2024](https://www.forbes.com/sites/josipamajic/2024/10/07/hacking-the-app-store-gen-zs-15m-arr-bootstrapped-success-story/)).

**The hook/format:** the creator, in their own established voice, hits a real problem they already talk about ("she left me on read"), then shows the app solving it. It is not a demo video with a creator attached — it is **the creator's normal content, with the app as the resolution.**

**Why it worked:** micro-creators in a niche have **higher trust and near-zero price**. Two $50 bets is a $100 experiment with a 200,000-download upside. This is the only tactic in this document that a studio with literally no budget can run **today**.

**Where are they now:** Umax still shipping (v1.3.5, updated Apr 2026). Anderson has stepped back from operating the apps; people from his teams have gone on to other 8-figure app businesses (his claim, [X, Aug 2025](https://x.com/blakeandersonw/status/1959308118250139745)). Alive.

**Ethical flag:** Umax's category — rating teenage boys' faces — has drawn psychologists' warnings about worsening the youth mental-health crisis ([Fortune via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/looksmaxxing-apps-rate-teen-boys-163942148.html)). We're documenting the *distribution tactic*, not endorsing the product.

## 4. Locket Widget — one founder video, 2M signups in two weeks

**What it is:** home-screen widget that shows photos from a small circle of friends.
**Funding at the time that mattered:** **bootstrapped side project.** Matt Moss, 22, built it as a gift for his girlfriend. (He raised $2.5M friends-and-family, then $10M led by Sam Altman with Mike Krieger participating, in **April 2022 — after** the viral moment.)

**What they posted.** Moss posted a **plain UI walkthrough of the app** on Locket's own TikTok account. Not a skit, not a trend, not a sound. Just: here is the thing, here is what it does.

**The numbers** ([TechCrunch, 11 Jan 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/01/11/locket-an-app-for-sharing-photos-to-friends-homescreens-hits-the-top-of-the-app-store); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90712709/locket-the-1-app-in-apples-app-store-uses-a-trick-hiding-in-plain-sight)):
- Launched **1 January 2022**
- Founder's walkthrough video: **~100,000 views in a couple of days**
- That seeded organic posts by others → **~2 million signups within two weeks**
- **#1 on the App Store in 30+ countries**
- Won Apple's **2022 App Store Cultural Impact Award**
- ~2 billion photos sent

**Why it worked — and this is the mechanic to internalise.** The product's value is **only realisable with other people**. Every install created pressure to recruit 3–5 friends. TikTok supplied the top of funnel; the *product* supplied the loop. 100,000 views became 2M signups because the ratio wasn't views→installs, it was views→installs→**invitations**.

**CTA:** none, really. The video was the CTA. The app name was the only thing to remember.

**Where are they now:** **Still alive and substantial.** Sensor Tower estimates ~**800,000 downloads and ~$300,000 revenue in the month** as of ~March 2026 ([Sensor Tower app overview](https://app.sensortower.com/overview/1600525061?country=US) — modelled estimate, not actual). Four years past the viral spike and still doing meaningful volume. The rare case that didn't churn out.

**Caveat:** the January-2022 TikTok is not the January-2026 TikTok. Small-account average views fell ~59% YoY in 2025 (see `algorithm.md` §4). A single walkthrough is much less likely to catch now.

## 5. Adam Lyttle — the boring, replicable indie case (closest to us)

**What it is:** a portfolio of 40–50+ small iOS apps, built solo.
**Funding:** **Bootstrapped, no VC.** Australian solo dev, started after a failed web-design business and significant debt.

**What he posts.** His stated strategy is **"become your own influencer"** — build the audience yourself rather than rent it. Worked example: to promote a Pokémon-card scanner app, he **drew a Pokémon card every day and posted it**. The content was the hobby, not the app.

**His framing of the maths** ([Adam Lyttle Apps newsletter](https://adamlyttleapps.substack.com/p/free-growth-hacks-to-boost-your-app)):
> "Sites like TikTok give your videos an automatic reach of 200 targeted viewers... You only need to get 1% of those viewers downloading your app, and that's 2 downloads per video."

**Numbers** ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/adam-lyttle-apps-breakdown), [adamlyttleapps.com](https://adamlyttleapps.com/)) — all founder-reported:
- 50+ apps built; one app per month for ~4 years
- Grew from **1,000 → 30,000 downloads/month** using free channels
- **$600K → $800K** in cumulative App Store sales (the jump credited to a paywall change, not TikTok)
- One app reached ~**$11,000/month** and **sold for $224,000**
- $100,000+ revenue in a single recent year

**Why this is the most relevant case for App Factory.** No creator budget. No viral moment. No exit. Just: many small apps, daily content in the *hobby community adjacent to each app*, compounding. The "2 downloads per video" maths is unglamorous and honest — and at 3 posts/day across a portfolio it is a real, if slow, install engine.

**What he says did NOT work:** his Reddit tactics eventually backfired — *"the downside was getting found out"* — i.e. non-native promotion in communities that punish it. Directly relevant to `credibility.md`.

**Where are they now:** actively shipping, publishing a podcast and newsletter. Alive, modest, sustainable.

## 6. The creator-seeding agency model (Playkit) — what "at volume" actually costs

Included as a **benchmark for what industrial-scale looks like**, not as a recommendation. `[ALL FIGURES AGENCY SELF-REPORTED — unverified]`

Source: [The Playbook by Suzanna — How 150+ creators and 600 TikToks per month become an acquisition engine](https://theplaybookbysuzanna.substack.com/p/how-150-creators-and-600-tiktoks)

- Network: **150–200 trained creators**
- Per campaign: **10 creators × 15 videos/week × 4 weeks = 600 videos**
- Three formats, in order of use: (1) **face-to-camera with captions explaining the pain point**, (2) **hook + product demo** (problem → solution), (3) **conversational "voice note to a friend"** (used least)
- Typical arc: week 1 ≈ 1,000 views/video → week 2 first 10k+ breakout → week 3 multiple hits → week 4 compounding
- Claimed aggregate: 500+ companies, **180M+ views, 1M+ app downloads in seven months**
- **Conversion happens in the comment section**, where creators answer questions as peers rather than pitching

**The number that should recalibrate us: 600 videos per app per month is the professional baseline.** A studio posting 3/day per app is at ~90/month — 15% of that. That is fine, but we should not benchmark ourselves against agency-scale outcomes.

# FAILURES AND FLAMEOUTS

## F1. Tube2Blog — 1,000,000 views, zero reported conversions

**The canonical "viral but not converting" case, with numbers.**

Louis Tromans posted TikTok content for **Tube2Blog**, a content-marketing/SaaS tool. Documented on Indie Hackers ([250K views in 28 days](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/spread-like-wildfire/250k-views-on-tiktok-in-28-days--M4ifFTiqk8-7Mw54rz2)):

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Views in first 28 days | **250,000** |
| Final reported views | **1,000,000** |
| Likes | 100,000 |
| Followers gained | 6,000 |
| **Conversions / signups reported** | **None** |

Content was "niche-oriented videos around my product" mixed with clips of famous people talking about marketing generally. When a commenter asked which formats outperformed, no data was shared.

**Diagnosis — three separate failures, any one of which is fatal:**
1. **Wrong audience.** A B2B content-marketing tool. TikTok's consumer, mass-market, impulse-purchase audience (see `audience.md` §3, Tier C) does not buy SaaS seats off a scroll.
2. **No product to convert into.** The founder said conversion would happen *"once the BETA is ready."* A million views were spent on a thing nobody could buy. **Views do not bank.**
3. **Content aimed at the wrong intent.** Clips of famous people discussing marketing attract people who like marketing content — not people with the specific problem Tube2Blog solves. High views, zero intent.

**Lesson for us:** never run a TikTok push before the App Store listing is live and the install path works end to end. And never post content whose audience is "people interested in the general topic" instead of "people with this specific problem right now."

## F2. LifePilot — 8 videos, 2,000 views, 11 downloads, and a one-line bug that killed it

Indie Hackers, [19 May 2026](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/day-10-11-downloads-2-000-tiktok-views-and-the-mistake-that-killed-my-us-conversions-1dcfbbeff5) — LifePilot (AI planner, iOS), launched 9 May 2026, day-10 report:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| TikTok videos posted | 8 |
| Total views | ~2,000 (~250/video) |
| Engagement | 12 likes, **0 comments** |
| App Store impressions | 182 |
| Store conversion rate | 9% |
| **Total downloads** | **11** |

**The killer:** the App Store listing's primary language was set to **Italian**, so US visitors hit a wall of Italian text and bounced.

**Diagnosis:**
1. **The funnel broke downstream of TikTok.** TikTok delivered ~250 views/video — which is *below but near* the ~500 median (Buffer, 11.4M posts). It was working roughly as expected. The loss happened on the store page.
2. **Volume far too low to learn anything.** 8 videos is not a test. Buffer's data says the median post gets ~500 views and the payoff is in the 90th percentile — you need dozens of posts before the distribution tells you anything.
3. **Zero comments = zero signal.** No comments means no one had a question, which means nobody was close to installing. Comment count is a better early conversion proxy than view count.
4. **No social proof.** Zero reviews on a brand-new listing.

**Lesson for us:** instrument the *whole* funnel before posting — views → profile visits → link clicks → **App Store impressions → store conversion rate** → installs. Store conversion rate is the number TikTok can't tell you and the number that most often kills indie apps. Check the listing in every target storefront.

## F3. Poparazzi — 6.2M installs, ~2,000 monthly actives

**Viral acquisition with no retention. VC-backed** ($15M Series A, mid-2022), included because the failure mode is universal.

- Hit **#1 free on the US App Store, May 2021**
- **6.2M lifetime installs**; peak ~**4M MAU**
- By shutdown: **~2,000–3,000 MAU** — a >99% collapse from peak
- Shut down **1 May 2023**, users given until 30 June 2023 to export
- Team of 15, "over two years" of runway claimed at raise; gone in under one
- Sources: [TechCrunch, 1 May 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/05/01/once-hot-photo-sharing-social-app-poparazzi-is-shutting-down), [LA Business Journal](https://labusinessjournal.com/technology/social-app-poparazzi-slowdown-after-fast-start/)

**Diagnosis:** the app was a **novelty, not a habit.** Social-graph apps need a reason to reopen on day 8. The virality was real and the top-of-funnel worked perfectly — which is exactly why it's instructive. **Every dollar of the $15M was spent on a retention problem that acquisition had disguised.**

**Lesson for us:** a viral TikTok moment measures how good the *pitch* is, not how good the *product* is. Before chasing reach, know day-7 and day-30 retention. If D7 is bad, a viral hit converts our one shot at attention into a permanently burned audience.

## F4. Gas — 7.4M installs, sold in 5 months, dead in 9 more

Nikita Bier's anonymous-compliments app for teens.

- Launched **Aug 2022**; **1M DAU within two months**; briefly out-ranked TikTok itself on the App Store
- **7.4M installs, ~$7M consumer spend** lifetime
- **Acquired by Discord, 17 Jan 2023** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/01/17/discord-acquires-gas-a-compliments-based-social-media-app-for-teens))
- **Shut down by Discord ~Oct/Nov 2023** after growth stalled post-acquisition ([The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/discord-shutters-gas-making-it-two-in-a-row-for-gas-founder))
- [Wikipedia: Gas (app)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_(app))

**Diagnosis:** cohort-based virality with a hard ceiling. Gas grew school-by-school; once a school saturated, the loop had nowhere to go and the novelty decayed. The exit was a *good outcome for the founder* and a **complete failure as a durable product**.

**Lesson for us:** distinguish "growth loop" from "novelty cascade." A cascade looks identical to a loop for about eight weeks. Ask: what makes user #1,000,001 join *after* the trend passes? If the answer is only "their friends did," it's a cascade.

## F5. NGL — 200M users, then the FTC

Anonymous Q&A app, launched Nov 2021, grew explosively on TikTok/Instagram teen networks. **Reached 200M users by March 2024.**

**Then, 9 July 2024:** FTC + LA District Attorney action. **$5M total settlement** ($4.5M consumer redress + $500K civil penalty), and NGL was **banned from offering anonymous messaging to under-18s** and required to implement a neutral age gate. — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/07/ftc-order-will-ban-ngl-labs-its-founders-offering-anonymous-messaging-apps-kids-under-18-halt), [FTC business blog](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2024/07/anonymous-messaging-app-targeting-teens-read-disturbing-allegations-ftc-los-angeles-da-action), [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2024/07/09/tech/ftc-ngl-anonymous-messaging-app-teens)

Alleged conduct: **fake messages sent by the app itself** to bait users into paying to "reveal" senders; **recurring $9.99/week charges** users believed were one-time; false claims of "world class AI content moderation."

**Diagnosis:** the growth tactics that made NGL enormous on TikTok — manufactured engagement, dark-pattern subscriptions, teen targeting — were the same tactics that got its core business banned. The regulatory risk **was** the growth strategy.

**Lesson for us:** TikTok's highest-converting mechanics for young audiences (fake urgency, ambiguous weekly subs, minors) carry existential regulatory tail risk. A bootstrapped studio has no legal budget. **The FTC does not care that we were only doing what worked.**

# Cross-cutting patterns

**What every win had:**
1. A **product legible in ≤3 seconds on a phone screen** (Cal AI's photo→number; Locket's widget; Umax's face→score)
2. A **cheap, impulsive price** — $30/year or ~$4–7/week, no exceptions
3. **Someone credible saying it in their own voice** — founder or micro-creator, never a brand voiceover
4. A working install path **before** the traffic arrived
5. **The comment section worked as the link**, because organic TikTok has no clickable CTA

**What every failure had at least one of:**
1. **Wrong audience** — B2B/consideration purchase on an impulse platform (Tube2Blog)
2. **Broken funnel downstream** of TikTok (LifePilot's Italian store listing)
3. **No retention behind the acquisition** (Poparazzi, Gas)
4. **Too little volume to learn** (LifePilot: 8 videos)
5. **Nothing to convert into** at the moment of peak attention (Tube2Blog's unshipped beta)
6. **Growth mechanics that were themselves the liability** (NGL)

**The cheapest tactic that worked, ranked by cost:**
1. **$100** — Blake Anderson's two $50 micro-creators → 200K downloads in a week
2. **$0** — Locket's founder posting a plain UI walkthrough
3. **$0, slow** — Adam Lyttle's "become your own influencer": daily content in the hobby adjacent to the app
4. **$4,000+/campaign** — Quittr's per-view creator deals with usage rights for Spark Ads
5. **$100k+/month** — Cal AI's 250-creator retainer network

Numbers 1–3 are available to us this week.
