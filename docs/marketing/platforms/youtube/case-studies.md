# YouTube: Case Studies — What Worked, What Didn't, Where They Are Now

**Research date:** 2026-08-05
**Selection rule:** minimum 4 cases, minimum 3 bootstrapped/no-VC, minimum 2 failures. Figures are cited; where a number is a founder's own unaudited claim I label it as such.

**Headline finding, stated up front.** The YouTube evidence is the mirror image of Instagram's. On Instagram, every verified win was *paid creator seeding*. On YouTube, every verified win was **organic content — either the founder's own channel or an unpaid third party's video.** Nobody in this set bought their way in. That is the strongest single argument for YouTube as a bootstrapped studio's channel.

---

## WIN 1 — HabitKit / Sebastian Roehl (bootstrapped solo, no VC) — the third-party review

**What it is:** a habit tracker with a pixel-grid visual. Built solo by Sebastian Roehl in a small town north of Cologne, Germany, after quitting his job.

**Numbers**
| Metric | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| MRR reached mid-2024 | $10,000 | https://sebastianroehl.substack.com/p/2024-my-indie-app-business-year-in |
| December 2024 | "my most successful month since I started the business" | same |
| 2025 total revenue (portfolio) | **$602,000** | https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/602k-revenue-solo-indie-hacker-app-portfolio-breakdown-2026 |
| Recurring monthly revenue | ~$28,000 from ~25,100 subscribers at $1–2/mo | same |
| Outside funding | $0 | https://sebastianroehl.substack.com/p/2024-my-indie-app-business-year-in |

**What actually moved the needle — his own words:**

> **YouTube (MKBHD feature)**: "led to a huge spike in downloads and revenue" in December. "I am still shocked at how much this video has impacted my numbers."

**Format / hook:** he made none of it. HabitKit appeared in a Marques Brownlee video. The install driver was one mention inside long-form content from a channel with enormous high-trust reach.

**What failed in the same year, for contrast — also his own words:** he won a RevenueCat contest for a **New York Times Square billboard** in June 2024. His verdict: *"in terms of downloads and revenue, this wasn't a huge success."* He also published 42+ articles and concluded *"growing this newsletter has turned out to be super hard."*

**Why the YouTube mention worked and the billboard didn't:** the billboard bought impressions from people with no relevant intent and no way to act. The YouTube mention delivered a warm recommendation to an audience already in a "what apps should I use" frame of mind, with a tappable link one scroll away. **Intent and adjacency, not reach.**

**Where are they now:** still solo, still bootstrapped, $602k in 2025, HabitKit still the flagship.

**Transferable?** Directly. Getting an app in front of tech-review and app-recommendation channels costs nothing but outreach. **This is the single highest-ROI, lowest-cost YouTube action available to us and it does not require running a channel at all.** Build a target list of 50 mid-size channels that do "apps I use" content and pitch them.

## WIN 2 — Adam Lyttle (bootstrapped solo, no VC) — the founder channel as a ranking flywheel

**What it is:** 40+ iOS apps plus three SaaS tools for app makers (App Gallery, Prelauncher, Make App Ads). Melbourne, Australia. Documented recovery from ~$200k of debt.

**Numbers**
| Metric | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Cumulative App Store revenue | crossed **$1M** | https://adamlyttleapps.com/ |
| Monthly revenue | ~$50,000 (~$600k/yr) | https://www.starterstory.com/adam-lyttle-apps-breakdown |
| Apps shipped | 40–50+, ~one per month | same |
| Pricing | $4.99–$7.99/week; ~$29.99/year | same |
| X followers | 56,600 | https://x.com/adamlyttleapps |
| Outside funding | $0 | https://www.starterstory.com/adam-lyttle-apps-breakdown |

**The claim that matters** — from his own video *"Why EVERY indie app dev should be on YouTube"* (published 28 January 2026, authorship confirmed via YouTube's oEmbed API):

> Starting a YouTube channel took him from **$10k/month to $70k/month** — not through direct sales from the videos, but by boosting **App Store download velocity**, which lifted his organic App Store rankings.

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L0LovslxRG8

**Label: `[ANECDOTAL]` — founder's unaudited claim.** No third party has verified the $10k→$70k figure and it partially conflicts with Starter Story's ~$50k/month. Treat the *mechanism* as the insight, not the multiplier.

**Why the mechanism is credible.** App Store ranking algorithms weight download velocity. A YouTube video that sends a concentrated burst of installs in a short window can push an app up a keyword ranking, and the ranking then delivers ongoing organic installs long after the video's traffic decays. **The video's direct installs are the smaller half of the return; the ranking lift is the larger half.** This is the most important non-obvious mechanic in this document, and it means YouTube view counts systematically *understate* YouTube's contribution.

**Format / hook:** honest-numbers indie-dev content — ASO keyword tactics, app critiques, revenue breakdowns, build sprints.

**Where are they now:** still shipping ~one app/month, still bootstrapped, channel active.

**The caveat that must be stated.** His YouTube audience is **other indie developers**, not the users of his fish-identifier and piano apps. The channel works via the velocity mechanism and via his SaaS-for-app-makers products, where the audience *is* the customer. If our app's user is a gym-goer, a build-in-public channel will not deliver gym-goers — see `audience.md` §6.

## WIN 3 — Obsidian (bootstrapped, no VC, no ads, no telemetry)

**What it is:** local-first markdown note-taking / PKM. Launched 2020 by Shida Li and Erica Xu (previously Dynalist).

**Numbers**
| Metric | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Users | 1,000,000+ | https://www.operatorbook.dev/stories/obsidian-revenue-estimates-2m-to-25m |
| Team | fewer than 20 | same |
| Outside funding | **$0** | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obsidian_(software) |
| Paid advertising | **none** | https://www.operatorbook.dev/stories/obsidian-revenue-estimates-2m-to-25m |
| Revenue | **never published by the company.** Public estimates span an order of magnitude: ~$2M ARR (https://getlatka.com/companies/obsidian.md) to ~$25M (https://finance.biggo.com/news/iVboYp0Bga3fZL9MJEv_) | — |

**Be explicit about the uncertainty:** Obsidian has never disclosed revenue. Anyone quoting a precise figure is guessing. The *structural* facts — bootstrapped, no ads, tiny team, 1M+ users — are solid.

**Format / hook:** Obsidian's growth ran through the **personal-knowledge-management YouTube community** — long-form videos by creators demonstrating workflows, vault structures and plugin systems. Not made by Obsidian. Made by users showing their systems.

**Why it worked:**
1. **The product is unshowable in 15 seconds and fully showable in 20 minutes.** A PKM system is a *workflow*. Long-form is the only format that can express it. This app category is structurally impossible on Instagram and structurally native to YouTube.
2. **Search intent is enormous and evergreen.** "Obsidian vs Notion," "how to build a second brain," "Obsidian plugins" — permanently searched queries.
3. **Users made the content because the content was itself a form of self-expression.** Showing your vault is showing your mind. That's a share loop, on the platform where the loop's natural format lives.

**Where are they now:** still bootstrapped, still no investors, still the reference product in its category, expanding into AI-adjacent knowledge tooling.

**Transferable?** The lesson: **if your app is a workflow, YouTube long-form isn't one option among several — it's the only channel that can carry the pitch.** And the ideal version isn't your channel, it's your users'. Design for showable workflows.

## WIN 4 — Marc Lou (bootstrapped, no VC) — 138k subscribers, $215/month, and why that's a success

**What it is:** a portfolio of ~15 small products. ShipFast (Next.js boilerplate), CodeFast (course), DataFast (analytics), and a dozen smaller ones.

**Numbers** — from his own public July 2025 breakdown (https://x.com/marclou/status/1950900252481130634):

| Line item | July 2025 revenue |
|---|---|
| CodeFast | $20,600 |
| ShipFast | $18,200 |
| DataFast | $6,100 |
| Twitter/X | $1,500 |
| Indie Page | $1,200 |
| **YouTube** | **$215** |
| Others (Zenvoice, LaunchViral, ByeDispute, HabitsGarden, WorkbookPDF, PoopUp) | <$500 combined |
| **Total** | **$48,921** |

| Metric | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube subscribers | 138,000 (from ~43k mid-2025) | https://nomadicblueprint.com/case-studies/marc-louvion |
| 2025 total revenue | **$1,032,000** | https://newsletter.marclou.com/p/i-made-1-032-000-in-2025 |
| Cumulative revenue | $2.26M+ | https://indiepattern.com/stories/marc-lou/ |
| Outside funding | $0 | same |

**Read the table carefully. This is the most useful single artefact in the document.**

A channel with **138,000 subscribers produced $215 of direct YouTube revenue in a month** — 0.4% of his income. Meanwhile the two products that channel sells generated $38,800 in the same month.

**Why this is a win, not a failure:** the channel is not a revenue line, it is the **distribution mechanism for the revenue lines**. Judging it by AdSense would be judging a shop window by the rent it collects. He tripled his subscriber base in 2025 while shipping $1M+ in product revenue.

**Why it's here as a warning too:** if the App Factory ever starts measuring a YouTube channel by its own monetisation, we will kill a working channel. **The metric is installs and revenue in the products, not RPM.** See `algorithm.md` §9 on why Shorts RPM in particular is a rounding error.

**Where are they now:** still solo, still bootstrapped, 2025 was ~20% *down* on 2024 by his own account — which he framed as a deliberate trade for better work/life balance and more diversified revenue. Worth noting: even the best-documented indie YouTube flywheel doesn't grow monotonically.

## WIN 5 — Screen Studio / Adam Pietrasiak (bootstrapped, no VC) — the product *is* the demo

**What it is:** a macOS screen-recording tool that auto-generates polished zooms and pans.

**Numbers**
| Metric | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| First month revenue | ~$30,000 | https://www.starterstory.com/screen-studio-breakdown |
| Customers in 9 months | ~8,000 | same |
| Single day (4 Jan 2023) | $3,467.45 | https://x.com/pie6k/status/1610782639841226752 |
| Model | pay-once, use-forever | https://marketingstrategybreakdowns.substack.com/p/screen-studio-moats-viralish-loops |
| Outside funding | $0 | same |

**Format / hook:** every video demo made *with* Screen Studio is an ad *for* Screen Studio. Creators and founders posting product demos on YouTube and X were unknowingly running the marketing campaign. Pietrasiak built in public, which seeded the first wave.

**Why it worked:** a genuine embedded distribution loop — the output is visibly distinctive, so viewers ask what made it. This is the YouTube-native equivalent of Hevy's in-product social loop.

**Where are they now:** still bootstrapped, still growing, still competing against VC-funded rivals (Loom, Descript) on a one-time-payment model.

**Transferable?** Only for tools whose output is itself distributed. But the general principle transfers: **ask whether the app's output can carry a visible signature into someone else's content.** That's free distribution forever.

# FAILURES

## FAILURE 1 — The dusty SaaS channel: two years, 30 subscribers, no views

**What happened:** the modal outcome for company YouTube channels. A documented example: a channel two years old with **30 subscribers and effectively no views**, which the author describes as the standard state — *"Most SaaS companies have a dusty YouTube channel sitting in a corner with a handful of subscribers and no views."*

Source: https://thoughtlytics.substack.com/p/44-reviving-a-dead-youtube-channel

**Diagnosis — three specific, avoidable errors:**

1. **Feature-announcement content has zero search demand.** Nobody types "[your app] v2.3 release notes." Every video was addressed to an audience that didn't exist yet. The fix is to make content addressed to the *problem*, not the product — YouTube's own guidance is *"Focus on what your audience likes"* (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225).
2. **Cadence used as a substitute for demand research.** YouTube states explicitly that *"growth in views across uploads is not correlated with time between uploads"* (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805). Posting weekly into a zero-demand niche produces zero views weekly. **Instagram rewards volume; YouTube does not.** An agent pipeline tuned for Instagram throughput will produce exactly this failure on YouTube.
3. **No thumbnail/title testing.** With CTR's published middle-50% band at 2–10% (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154), a sub-2% CTR channel is a thumbnail failure that Test & Compare would surface in two weeks — free, and never used.

---

## FAILURE 2 — The templated-content trap: demonetised and de-recommended by design

**What happened:** YouTube formalised the killing of high-volume templated output. Renamed "repetitious content" → **"inauthentic content"** on 15 July 2025, then on 16 July 2026 defined three demonetisable categories:

1. **Generic, repetitive, or template-based content** — "easily made with AI, CGI, or templates with minimal variation," explicitly including tutorials reproducing already-prevalent content.
2. **Off-putting or distressing content** engineered to manipulate emotion for views.
3. **AI personas discussing sensitive topics** (finance, legal, healthcare, medical).

Channels with excessive amounts of any category lose YPP eligibility. **"High-quality content enhanced by AI that demonstrates creativity and originality" remains fine.**

Sources: https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/ · https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/09/youtube-prepares-crackdown-on-mass-produced-and-repetitive-videos-as-concern-over-ai-slop-grows/ · https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392

**Why this is the App Factory's single largest platform risk.** Our structural advantage — an agent pipeline producing many videos from one source — is a near-perfect description of category 1 if executed naively. Thirty Shorts cut from the same template, same voice, same structure, differing only in which feature is shown, is what this policy was written about.

**The defence is not tooling, it's variance.** Founder-supplied original footage is necessary but not sufficient; the *narrative* has to vary too. See `credibility.md`.

---

## FAILURE 3 — Views ≠ revenue: the $215 line item

Covered in Win 4 and repeated here because it's the failure hiding inside a success.

**138,000 subscribers → $215/month of YouTube revenue.**

If YouTube is treated as a media business, this is a catastrophic result. It is only a success because the channel feeds products. **Any App Factory channel must have a named downstream conversion before the first video ships.** A channel without one is a hobby with a production budget.

---

## FAILURE 4 — Impressions without intent: the Times Square billboard

Sebastian Roehl won a Times Square billboard for HabitKit in June 2024. His assessment: *"in terms of downloads and revenue, this wasn't a huge success"* — in the same year the MKBHD mention drove his best month ever.

Source: https://sebastianroehl.substack.com/p/2024-my-indie-app-business-year-in

**Diagnosis:** a billboard delivers maximum reach and zero intent, zero adjacency and zero call-to-action affordance. A YouTube mention delivers a warm recommendation to a self-selected audience with a clickable link. **This is the cleanest natural experiment in either document: same app, same year, same founder — reach lost decisively to intent.**

Generalised: distrust any channel whose primary offer is impressions. Both YouTube search and a trusted creator's endorsement offer intent instead.

## What the whole set actually says

1. **Every YouTube win here was organic — founder channel or unpaid third party.** Zero paid seeding. The opposite of the Instagram set.
2. **The highest-ROI action isn't running a channel — it's getting into someone else's video.** One MKBHD mention beat a year of a solo founder's own content output.
3. **YouTube's contribution is systematically under-measured.** The App Store download-velocity → ranking-lift mechanism (Win 2) means installs continue long after the video's traffic decays. Attribution windows will miss this.
4. **Cadence is not the lever it is on Instagram.** YouTube states so explicitly. Our throughput advantage converts poorly here; demand research and thumbnail testing convert well.
5. **The channel must have a named downstream product conversion.** $215/month from 138k subscribers is what YouTube pays you directly.
6. **Workflow-shaped products are structurally advantaged.** Obsidian's entire growth story is unavailable on Instagram at any budget.
7. **Templated output is a documented, enforced failure mode as of July 2026.**

## What I could not verify

- Adam Lyttle's $10k→$70k/month claim is unaudited and conflicts with a third-party estimate of ~$50k/month. The mechanism is credible; the multiplier isn't confirmed.
- Obsidian's revenue has never been disclosed; estimates span $2M–$25M.
- No published dataset gives YouTube-video → app-install conversion rates. The RevenueCat 2026 report (115,000 apps, published 19 March 2026) has excellent subscription benchmarks but **discloses no acquisition-channel breakdown** — I checked specifically.
- Screen Studio's current ARR is not public.
