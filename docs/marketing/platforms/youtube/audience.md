## 2. Intent level: this is the whole point

**YouTube is the only major social platform with a genuine demand-capture surface.** Search is a first-class discovery mode, and unlike Instagram search it is a primary user behaviour, not a minority one. YouTube.com's 53 billion monthly visits are substantially driven by people arriving with a question.

Three distinct surfaces with three distinct intent levels:

| Surface | Intent | What performs |
|---|---|---|
| **Search** | High — user typed a problem | "How do I...", "best X for Y", "X vs Y", comparison and tutorial content. Evergreen: compounds for years. |
| **Suggested / Up Next** | Medium — user is already in a topic | Deeper cuts, sequels, adjacent problems. Feeds off the search asset. |
| **Browse / Home** | Low — user is browsing | Personality, novelty, series. Closest to Instagram's model. |
| **Shorts feed** | Zero — user is scrolling | Same interruption model as Reels (`algorithm.md` §1). |

**For an app studio this is the decisive structural fact.** On Instagram you must manufacture demand. On YouTube you can also *harvest* it. A single well-ranked video answering "how to track calories without weighing food" installs users every week for two years without further work. Nothing on Instagram does that.

The cost is speed. Search-driven growth is slow to start and compounds; Reels-driven growth is fast to start and decays.

## 3. Where the niches actually concentrate

YouTube's audience organises around *problems and interests*, not aesthetic identity. That's the sorting principle, and it inverts Instagram's vertical map.

**Strongly concentrated on YouTube:**

| Vertical | Why it lives here | App implication |
|---|---|---|
| Software, dev tools, technical workflows | The entire "how do I do X in Y" corpus lives here. | Dev tools, IDE plugins, CLI utilities, API products, self-hosted software. |
| Productivity systems / PKM | Long-form is the only format that can show a *system*. | Note-taking, task managers, second-brain apps. Obsidian was built here (see `case-studies.md`). |
| Personal finance & investing | Considered decisions; people research. | Budgeting, tax, portfolio apps. Also the highest-RPM niche. |
| Gaming | Native to the platform since inception. | Games, companion apps, mods. |
| Creator tooling (video, audio, streaming) | The audience produces content and needs tools. | Editors, screen recorders, thumbnail tools, asset libraries. |
| Tech reviews & gadgets | Enormous, high-trust, high-conversion. | Any consumer app that can earn a mention in a review video (see HabitKit/MKBHD in `case-studies.md`). |
| Education & test prep | Search-driven by definition. | Study, flashcard, language apps. |
| Health, fitness *explanation* (vs. fitness *aesthetics*) | "Why" content rather than "look" content. | Same app categories as Instagram, different content register. |
| Home, DIY, repair, hobbies | Tutorial-shaped demand. | Niche utility apps. |

**Weakly concentrated on YouTube (relative to Instagram):**

| Vertical | Why not |
|---|---|
| Fashion, beauty aesthetics, "looks" | Present, but Instagram/TikTok own the discovery loop. |
| Impulse-purchase lifestyle apps | No search demand for a product nobody knew existed. |
| Trend-driven / meme-native apps | The half-life is shorter than YouTube's discovery cycle. |

## 4. App categories that convert on YouTube — and why

The converting pattern on YouTube has different properties from Instagram's:

1. **The app solves a problem someone would type into a search box.** If nobody searches for it, YouTube's best surface is unavailable to you and you're competing on Browse with entertainment.
2. **The value takes more than 5 seconds to explain — and that's an advantage.** Complexity that kills a Reel is what justifies a 9-minute video.
3. **Price tolerance is higher.** A viewer who watched 6 minutes about a workflow will pay $15–50/month or a one-time $99. This is the opposite of Instagram's $5-impulse economics.
4. **Trust transfers from the demonstrator.** YouTube's format lets someone *use* your app on camera for minutes. That's the highest-fidelity proof available on any platform.

**Converts well:** developer tools, note-taking/PKM, productivity systems, screen-recording and creator tools, budgeting/finance, self-hosted software, study and language apps, niche professional utilities, anything with a "workflow."

**Converts poorly:** apps whose entire value is a 3-second visual reveal (there's nothing to fill 8 minutes with), trend-native apps, apps with no searchable problem statement, apps priced so low that the CAC of a long-form video can't be justified — though at $0 marginal production cost with agent editing, that last constraint largely dissolves for us.

### The test

> **If you can write ten search queries a stranger would plausibly type that your app is the honest answer to, YouTube is your platform.**

If you can't write ten, you don't have a search asset, and you should treat YouTube as a Shorts-only channel (which is fine — it just makes YouTube a second Instagram rather than a different thing).

## 5. Subscriber value: this compounds where Instagram's doesn't

`[DOCUMENTED]` YouTube states subscriber count "doesn't represent the number of viewers who watch your videos" (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805) — it is not a ranking multiplier.

But it *is* a distribution asset in a way Instagram followers no longer are:
- Subscribers generate Home and Subscriptions-feed impressions on every upload.
- The back catalogue stays discoverable and keeps converting.
- Search ranking is per-video and permanent, so an old video keeps acquiring subscribers who then see new videos.

Instagram followers, by contrast, are a decaying asset — Mosseri actively tells creators to stop optimising for them, and growth rates halved YoY in the Socialinsider data.

**Read:** YouTube is where a bootstrapped studio builds an owned-ish audience. Instagram is where it rents reach. Over 24 months the YouTube asset is worth more; over 8 weeks the Instagram one is.

## 6. Multi-app portfolio implications

YouTube handles a portfolio *better* than Instagram does, for a specific reason: **ranking is per-video, and a channel is not penalised for a video that underperforms** (documented, `algorithm.md` §7). A studio channel can host videos about five different apps, because each video is matched to its own searchers.

The viable structures:
1. **One studio/founder channel covering all apps** — works, because search intent routes viewers per-video. Best fit for the App Factory. The narrative through-line ("we build small apps") is itself content.
2. **One channel per app** — only worth it once an app has enough searchable surface area to sustain 30+ videos.
3. **Founder-face channel with build-in-public content** — highest-trust option, and the model behind Adam Lyttle and Marc Lou (`case-studies.md`). Note the important caveat: this audience is *other builders*, not your app's end users. It grows the studio's reputation and the App Store's download-velocity signal, not necessarily the target market.

That third distinction is the most commonly missed one and it deserves emphasis:

> **A build-in-public YouTube channel acquires an audience of builders. If your app's user is a gym-goer, that channel will not acquire gym-goers.** It may still be worth running for download velocity, credibility and studio deal flow — but it is not app marketing, and shouldn't be measured as such.

## 7. Where YouTube beats Instagram for us

1. **Search intent.** Demand capture, not demand creation. No Instagram equivalent.
2. **Evergreen compounding.** A video ranked in search installs users for years.
3. **Clickable links everywhere** — description, pinned comment, cards, end screens. No 3-hop bio-link funnel.
4. **"Viewed vs swiped away"** — a true hook-quality metric Instagram doesn't provide.
5. **Test & Compare** — native, free thumbnail/title A/B testing optimised for watch time.
6. **Documented safety of Shorts** — "Shorts can't hurt your Video performance," stated by YouTube.
7. **Higher price tolerance** — the format justifies considered purchases.
8. **Third-party leverage.** One mention in someone else's review video can outperform a year of your own posting (HabitKit/MKBHD, `case-studies.md`).

## 8. Where YouTube loses to Instagram for us

1. **Slow.** Search rankings and subscriber bases take months. Instagram gives a signal in 24 hours.
2. **Higher production floor.** A 7-minute video that holds retention is genuinely harder to assemble from raw screen recordings than a 20-second Reel. This is the App Factory's real constraint.
3. **Cadence doesn't buy reach.** YouTube states upload frequency isn't correlated with view growth — so agent throughput, our structural advantage, converts to less benefit here than on Instagram.
4. **Weaker fit for impulse-purchase consumer categories** — beauty, aesthetics, trend apps.
5. **Older median audience** — worse fit if the app targets under-25s.

## 9. The concrete call, per app category

| If the app targets... | YouTube? | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Developers, technical users | **Yes, first platform** | Their entire discovery behaviour is here. Instagram is useless. |
| Note-taking, PKM, productivity systems | **Yes, first platform** | Long-form is the only format that can show a system. Obsidian proves the model. |
| Budgeting, tax, investing (consumer) | **Yes** | Researched purchase, high search volume, high price tolerance. |
| Creator tools (editing, recording, thumbnails) | **Yes** | Audience is on-platform and the demo *is* the platform's own medium. |
| Study, flashcards, language learning | **Yes** | Search-driven; enormous evergreen query volume. |
| Self-hosted / open-source software | **Yes** | Community lives on YouTube + GitHub. |
| Fitness / calorie tracking | **Both** — YouTube for the "why," Instagram for the "look" | Different registers of the same category. Instagram converts faster. |
| Habit tracking, journaling | **Secondary** | Some search demand ("best habit tracker app") but the impulse case is stronger on IG. |
| Widgets, wallpapers, home-screen customisation | **Shorts only** | Almost no search demand; pure visual reveal. Treat as a second Reels feed. |
| Beauty, appearance, "looksmaxxing" | **No** | Wrong platform culture and wrong discovery loop. |
| Trend-native / meme apps | **No** | Discovery cycle is slower than the trend's half-life. |
| Anything targeting 13–20 | **Shorts only** | The long-form audience skews meaningfully older. |

## 10. The honest sequencing recommendation

For a bootstrapped studio with agent-editing capacity and founder screen recordings, the sequencing that follows from the evidence:

- **Weeks 0–8:** Instagram Reels + YouTube Shorts from the *same* edits. Same vertical asset, different upload. Marginal cost of the second upload is near zero, and YouTube's swipe-away metric will teach you more about hook quality than Instagram's insights will. Use Trial Reels for the fast read.
- **Weeks 4+:** start the searchable long-form library on YouTube, one video per week, targeting the ten queries your app honestly answers. Accept no traction for the first three months.
- **Ongoing:** the Instagram account is a rental that must keep paying rent weekly. The YouTube library is an asset that starts paying in month 4 and never stops.
