# Paid acquisition case studies — what actually happened, and where they are now

Research date: **2026-08-05**. Ten cases: **five that worked** (four of them bootstrapped
or near-bootstrapped) and **five that burned money**. Every figure is sourced or marked
unverifiable. Diagnosis follows each.

Claim tags: **[DOCUMENTED]** = primary/major-press source · **[ANECDOTAL]** = operator
self-report · **[UNVERIFIED]** = secondary source, no methodology.

**Survivorship warning up front:** four of the five successes below are from 2024–2026 AI
consumer apps or one-of-a-kind organic phenomena. The base rate for "bootstrapped studio
makes paid UA work" is *very low* — RevenueCat's 2026 data has median D60 revenue per
install at **$0.55 in North America** against a **$4.06 US Apple Ads CPI**, meaning the
median app cannot buy a single install profitably (see `landscape.md` §0). These are the
exceptions. Read them for mechanism, not for encouragement.

# PART ONE — WHAT WORKED

## 1. Cal AI — bootstrapped, creators first, ads second

**Status: bootstrapped, no outside funding. Acquired.**

### What they spent, and on what

| Period | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| May 2024 (month 1) | **$28,000 revenue** | [Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/case-study/cal-ai) **[UNVERIFIED]** |
| June 2024 | $115,000 revenue | ibid. |
| Sept 2024 | **$1M cumulative revenue**, ~4 months from launch | [Forbes, 2024-10-07](https://www.forbes.com/sites/josipamajic/2024/10/07/hacking-the-app-store-gen-zs-15m-arr-bootstrapped-success-story/) **[DOCUMENTED]** |
| 2025 | Growth engine: **250+ fitness creators on monthly retainer**, TikTok + Instagram; largest single creator paid *tens of thousands per month* for four posts; total marketing spend **mid-six-figures/month** | Forbes / [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/ben-sherry/he-built-an-ai-app-in-high-school-made-40m-and-sold-to-myfitnesspal-now-hes-aiming-even-bigger/91307748) **[DOCUMENTED]** |
| Late 2025 | ~**$30M annual run rate**; 17-person remote team; **zero outside funding** | Inc. **[DOCUMENTED]** |
| Jan 2026 | **$5.7M revenue in one month** against **$1M+/month in ads** across Meta, TikTok, Instagram | [Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/case-study/cal-ai) **[UNVERIFIED]** |
| — | **MrBeast integration cost $500K, returned ~$400K directly** | ibid. **[UNVERIFIED]** |

Pricing: **$2.49/month or $29.99/year** at exit (earlier reported as $10/mo, $30/yr in the
Oct 2024 Forbes piece — they moved down-market). 15M+ downloads. Reported ~30% retention,
described as exceptional for health apps. **[UNVERIFIED]**

### The creative that worked

Native creator content, not ads. Creators filmed themselves photographing meals; the
product demo *was* the hook. Paid performance ads only scaled **after** creator content had
established which hooks converted — and revenue grew from $2M/month (influencer ceiling) to
$5.7M/month once paid amplified proven creative. **[UNVERIFIED]** as to the exact
sequencing figures, but every source agrees on the ordering.

### The metric they actually ran on

Not CPI. Not LTV:CAC. **RPM vs CPM** — revenue per 1,000 views against cost per 1,000
views. Co-founder Blake Anderson: *"Ideally, you can build a product with a high RPM and a
low CPM."* ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/josipamajic/2024/10/07/hacking-the-app-store-gen-zs-15m-arr-bootstrapped-success-story/)) **[DOCUMENTED]**

They also ran paywall optimisation through Superwall, reporting **~10% conversion lift from
A/B testing improved animations**. **[DOCUMENTED via Forbes]**

### Payback / LTV:CAC

**Not disclosed.** Do not let anyone tell you otherwise — no source I found publishes Cal
AI's CAC or payback period. What *is* inferable: $1M/month ads against $5.7M/month revenue
is roughly **5.7× blended monthly ROAS**, but that mixes new and existing subscribers and
is not a payback figure. **[MARKED UNVERIFIABLE]**

### Where are they now

**Acquired by MyFitnessPal. Deal closed December 2025; announced March 2026.** Terms
undisclosed; the founder had earlier rejected offers at 1× profit. Cal AI operates
independently under MFP, integrated with MFP's 20M-item food database.
([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/ben-sherry/he-built-an-ai-app-in-high-school-made-40m-and-sold-to-myfitnesspal-now-hes-aiming-even-bigger/91307748),
[Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/case-study/cal-ai)) **[DOCUMENTED]**

### Diagnosis — what's transferable and what isn't

**Transferable:**
- **Creators before ads, always.** Paid amplified validated creative; it never discovered it.
- **RPM vs CPM sidesteps attribution entirely.** You don't need to know which install came
  from which video — only whether revenue per 1,000 delivered views beats cost per 1,000.
  At bootstrapped scale where SKAdNetwork gives you nothing (`landscape.md` §7), **this is
  the only workable measurement frame.**
- **Start with small creators across niches, scale the winners.** Explicitly their method.

**Not transferable:**
- The category. Calorie tracking is a **daily-habit, high-intent, mass-market** category
  with unusually high willingness to pay. Most of our portfolio will not be.
- The moment. 2024–25 AI-photo-recognition was a genuinely novel demo. "Point phone at
  food, get macros" is a *visual* hook that works in 3 seconds of vertical video. **Ask of
  every app we build: does it have a 3-second visual demo?** If not, the creator channel is
  structurally closed to it.
- The scale. $1M/month in ads is not a playbook we can copy; the *ordering* is.

## 2. Umax + RizzGPT (Blake Anderson) — bootstrapped, TikTok-native

**Status: bootstrapped, no outside funding.**

As of **2024-10-07**, Blake Anderson (23) and Zach Yadegari (17) had **three apps
collectively at $15.4M ARR** — Cal AI at $8M, with Umax and RizzGPT making up the balance.
Each app had 100k+ monthly users, at $1M–5M/year each.
([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/josipamajic/2024/10/07/hacking-the-app-store-gen-zs-15m-arr-bootstrapped-success-story/)) **[DOCUMENTED]**

### What they spent and the creative

TikTok influencer partnerships as the **primary distribution channel**, using the same
test-small-then-scale method and the same RPM/CPM discipline. Yadegari on an earlier
project: *"That took off, marketed it myself, just recording TikToks, blew up to 5 million
users."* — the founder was the first creator. **[DOCUMENTED]**

Umax's product (AI facial-attractiveness scoring) and RizzGPT's (AI dating-message
suggestions) are both **inherently shareable outputs** — the app generates a screenshot
that *is* the ad.

### Payback / LTV:CAC

**Not disclosed. [MARKED UNVERIFIABLE]**

### Where are they now

Verified only to **October 2024**. I could not source reliable current revenue or status
for Umax or RizzGPT as of 2026-08-05. **[UNVERIFIED — status unknown]** Note that both sit
in categories (looksmaxxing, dating "rizz") with high App Store policy risk and fast trend
decay; treat any assumption of continuity as unwarranted.

### Diagnosis

**The transferable insight is product design, not marketing.** These apps were built so
that *using them produces content*. The share asset is the output, not a screenshot of the
UI. For a factory that plans to market at volume with AI-assembled content, **"does using
this app generate something worth posting?" belongs in the stage-1 spec**, not the
marketing plan.

**The caution:** these are trend-surfing apps in policy-adjacent categories. Our cadence
guardrail (`playbooks/aso.md` §Cadence) exists precisely to stop us building this way. The
mechanism is worth stealing; the category selection is not.

## 3. Matt Corey — indie iOS dev, $10/day, Reddit vs Apple Search Ads

**Status: bootstrapped solo indie. The most directly relevant case in this document.**

### What he spent, and what happened

A solo developer with a personal-finance app moved his existing **$10/day** Apple Search
Ads budget to Reddit in **May 2025** and published the head-to-head
([*Rethinking my Indie App Advertising*](https://mattcorey.substack.com/p/rethinking-my-indie-app-advertising)). **[ANECDOTAL — real account, real money]**

| Metric | Apple Search Ads | Reddit |
|---|---|---|
| eCPM | $141.43 | **$0.47** |
| CPC | $1.59 | **$0.70** |
| CPA | $4.36 | **$0.82** |
| Day-1 impressions | ~75/day | **17,000+** |
| Day-1 clicks | 6/day | **59** |
| Day-1 installs | 4 | 4 |

He had also run **Google Ads for six months and got zero clicks.**

### The creative that worked

A simple screen recording of the app in use, with plain benefit copy:

> *"End the bill panic. Know what's due, what's safe to spend, and what's next."*

Problem-state → resolution → specificity. No production budget. **This is precisely the
asset the factory's founder-demo-recording pipeline already produces.**

### The honest result — this is a partial success, not a win

**Reddit's $0.82 CPA still exceeded his ARPU.** He could not scale it. Five-times-cheaper
installs that are still unprofitable are still unprofitable.

What he *did* get: measurable improvement in **organic App Store keyword rankings** from
the Reddit traffic, and clean attribution through App Store Connect's **"App Referrer"**
grouping — **no SKAdNetwork integration required.** **[ANECDOTAL]**

### Where are they now

Still an independent developer as of the May 2025 post. **Current status not verified.**
**[UNVERIFIED]**

### Diagnosis

Three lessons, all of which shape `triggers.md`:

1. **Apple Search Ads at $10/day is statistically inert.** 75 impressions and 6 clicks a
   day means you need *months* to accumulate a readable sample on any keyword. His
   description — *"a black hole"* — is the correct characterisation of ASA below roughly
   £10/day. **The floor is real: either spend enough to get 50–100 taps per keyword cluster,
   or don't run it.**
2. **Reddit is the cheapest place to buy volume for a read.** 17,000 impressions and 59
   clicks on day one for ~$10. Whatever else is true, **Reddit will tell you within a week
   whether your hook works.** That is worth £100 of anyone's money.
3. **A cheap CPA above your ARPU is still a losing channel.** He was 5× more efficient and
   still could not scale. **The binding constraint was monetisation, not media buying.**
   This is the single most important pattern in this whole document.

## 4. Yuka — bootstrapped, ~80M users, **zero paid marketing**

**Status: bootstrapped, profitable, never raised.**

Founded 2016 in France, launched 2017 (FR) and 2020 (US). **60M+ users** by most 2024–25
counts, **73–80M** in more recent sources
([US Chamber of Commerce interview with co-founder Julie Chapon](https://www.uschamber.com/co/good-company/the-leap/yuka-app-organic-growth)). **[DOCUMENTED]**
Revenue ~$20.3M ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/yuka.io)) — **[UNVERIFIED]**, Latka
figures are self-reported and unaudited.

**"The app hasn't invested in paid marketing or advertising."** Growth came entirely from
word of mouth. Monetisation is premium subscriptions and food guides — deliberately no
advertising and no producer relationships, because independence *is* the product promise.

### Where are they now

Operating, profitable, independent, still not buying media. **[DOCUMENTED]**

### Diagnosis — this is the control group, and it is the most important case here

Yuka is in this document as a **counterfactual**, and it does more work than the successes.

- The product produces a **public, repeatable, socially charged moment**: scan a product in
  a shop, get a red score, show someone. The sharing loop is a *core interaction*, not a
  share button bolted on.
- It is **useful in the presence of other people**, in a place people already are. That is
  the structural property that makes word of mouth free.
- **They never had to solve the attribution problem, the learning-phase problem, or the
  CPI-vs-LTV problem, because they never entered the auction.**

**The uncomfortable read for a bootstrapped studio: the highest-EV move is usually not
"spend better" — it's "build something with a Yuka-shaped loop."** Before approving any
paid budget, `triggers.md` asks whether the app has an organic loop at all. Yuka is why.

## 5. Reflectly — *funded*, UGC-driven paid, included as contrast

**Status: NOT bootstrapped — $5.97M raised across 4 rounds.** Included because the
mechanism is relevant and the origin story is near-bootstrapped.

Founded 2016 in Copenhagen. Launched on **founders' savings plus $250,000 from a single
angel** (Michael Mortensen)
([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/reflectly/__wsshogrPyXc3oWbnVxQpSUXv8csfyJ-MxqGjHRa0Djc),
[Forbes 2018](https://www.forbes.com/sites/heatherfarmbrough/2018/05/01/reflectly-wants-to-be-an-adidas-of-the-mind/)). **[DOCUMENTED]**

Growth reported as **70% organic**; their head of growth described adding **4M+ users in a
year** by **soliciting user-generated ad content** and letting empirical data direct
spend across channels and segments
([Nomad Podcast ep. 35](https://nomadpodcast.com/ep-35-darius-moravcik/)). **[ANECDOTAL]**

**Spend, CPI, payback: not disclosed anywhere I could find. [MARKED UNVERIFIABLE]**

### Where are they now

Still live and operating on both stores as of 2026-08-05. User figures in circulation range
wildly (500k in one profile, "tens of millions" in others) — **the public numbers for this
company are not reliable. [UNVERIFIED]**

### Diagnosis

Two things worth keeping:

- **Solicit UGC from your own users rather than buying it from creators.** For a journaling
  app, users already produce the content; the studio just has to ask and license. Cheapest
  possible creative pipeline.
- **70% organic even at scale.** Even the funded, paid-heavy case ran majority-organic.
  Paid was an amplifier on an organic base — a pattern that repeats in every success here.

# PART TWO — WHAT BURNED

## 6. Quibi — $1.75B raised, installs bought, retention absent

**The canonical "UA scaled installs but not retention" failure.**

- Raised **$1.75 billion** **[DOCUMENTED]**
- Launched April 2020 with an enormous marketing campaign; hit **#4 most-downloaded free
  iPhone app in the US** on day one; **~4.5M downloads** (Sensor Tower estimate)
- **90% of 90-day-free-trial signups cancelled before converting.** Only **~72,000 (≈8%)
  converted to paid**, per a third-party report Quibi publicly disputed as "incorrect by an
  order of magnitude"
  ([Deadline, July 2020](https://deadline.com/2020/07/quibi-converts-just-8-percent-of-free-trials-to-paid-subscriptions-new-report-says-1202981420/)) **[DOCUMENTED, contested by company]**
- Shut down roughly **six months after launch**

### Diagnosis

The failure was **not** in the media buying — the media buying worked perfectly. It
delivered a top-5 App Store chart position on day one. **The failure was that the product
had no retention, and paid acquisition faithfully converted cash into a number that looked
like success.**

Contributing causes, both instructive:
- **Mobile-only, no TV or desktop** — an artificial constraint on the core use case
- **Misread the category**: bet that consumers wanted professionally-produced short-form
  video, at a moment when they were already spending hours on algorithmic UGC

**The rule this generates for us:** *installs are not a success metric and must never
appear on a factory dashboard as one.* The analytics taxonomy already gets this right —
it specifies D1/D7/D30 retention **on the habit event, "not on app-open — opens lie."**
Quibi is what happens when a very well-funded organisation optimises the lying metric.

## 7. Lensa AI (Prisma Labs) — the viral spike that could not be held

**Not a budget-burn failure. A worse kind: a success that could not be retained.**

| Period | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Nov 2022 | **$70M+ revenue in one month** (company estimate) | [NPR/OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2023/01/20/a-rocky-past-haunts-the-mysterious-company-behind-the-lensa-ai-photo-app/) **[DOCUMENTED]** |
| Dec 2022 | **19.3M downloads**; **$30.7M consumer spend** | [Statista](https://statista.com/statistics/1350980/lensa-ai-in-app-revenue-worldwide) **[DOCUMENTED]** |
| Jan 2023 | Downloads collapse to **1.4M** | ibid. |
| Full-year 2023 | ~$18M total revenue | ibid. |
| May 2024 | **Under $600,000/month** | ibid. |

From $30.7M in a month to under $0.6M in a month — a **~98% decline in 17 months**.

### Diagnosis

The Magic Avatars feature was a **one-shot novelty purchase**, not a habit. Users paid
once, got their avatars, and had no reason to return. There was no repeat use case, so
there was no retention, so there was no LTV beyond the first transaction.

**Where the money would have burned:** any UA spend at Dec-2022 CPIs would have been priced
against Dec-2022 revenue-per-install, which was a **transient artefact of a viral moment**.
Scaling paid on those unit economics — which many companies did in that window — meant
buying installs at a CPI justified by an RPI that evaporated within eight weeks.

**The rule for us:** *never set a CPI ceiling from a spike.* Set it from **cohort revenue
at a fixed day-count (D30/D60) measured on a cohort acquired at least 60 days ago.**
Trailing-window RPI during a growth spike is the single easiest way to talk yourself into
unaffordable spend. See `triggers.md` §4.

## 8. IRL — $170M raised, 95% of users fake, founder charged

**The incentivised-install failure, taken to its criminal conclusion.**

- Raised **$170M** led by SoftBank and Peter Thiel in 2022; peak valuation **$1.2B** **[DOCUMENTED]**
- Claimed **20M active users** — the metric that secured the funding
- Board investigation found **>95% of accounts were "automated or from bots"**
  ([TechCrunch, 2023-06-26](https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/26/irl-shut-down-fake-users/),
  [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2023/06/25/irl-shutting-down-startup-admits-95-percent-of-messaging-app-users-were-fake/)) **[DOCUMENTED]**
- **SEC opened an investigation in December 2022**
- Prosecutors charged founder Abraham Shafi with defrauding investors, alleging he spent
  **millions on "incentive advertising" to inflate app installs** **[DOCUMENTED]**
- **Shut down June 2023**

### Diagnosis

Incentivised install networks — pay-per-install where the user is rewarded for installing —
produce **installs that are real in the postback and worthless in the product**. Because
install counts are the metric most easily inflated and most eagerly reported, this is the
default failure mode of any UA programme judged on volume.

Two mechanisms worth naming:
- **Incentivised traffic passes attribution.** Your MMP will happily report these as
  installs. Nothing in SKAdNetwork or AdServices distinguishes a bought install from a
  wanted one.
- **The only defence is a downstream metric.** Not installs, not registrations, not D1
  opens — the **activation event** (`playbooks/analytics-taxonomy.md`: "if you can't name
  the activation event, the spec isn't done").

**For a bootstrapped studio, the practical version of this risk isn't fraud — it's cheap
tier-3 geo traffic.** India CPI at $0.89 against IN/SEA D60 RPI of $0.11 (see
`landscape.md` §1) produces exactly the same outcome as incentivised installs: a big
number, no business. **Never optimise a campaign to CPI.**

## 9. The meditation app — ¥50,000 burned, and the rating went with it

**The indie-scale failure, and the one most likely to be *our* failure.**

An indie developer ran **¥50,000 (~£260) of Meta ads** for a meditation app. Result:
**500 installs, and the App Store rating fell from 4.5 to 3.2**
([PickAppDuck, 2026-03-12](https://note.com/pickappduck/n/naae8c552077f)). **[ANECDOTAL]**

**Cause:** targeting mismatch. The ads targeted "people worried about sleep"; the app
delivered general meditation content. *"A gap between expectations and reality was
created."*

### Diagnosis — this is the most expensive £260 in this document

The install cost was ~£0.52 — by CPI alone this looks like a **triumph**. It was a
disaster, because:

1. **The wrong users left the wrong reviews.** People who came for a sleep app and got a
   meditation app rated it honestly and badly.
2. **A 4.5→3.2 rating drop suppresses conversion on *every future install*, paid and
   organic.** Per `aso.md` §5: sub-3.5 stars materially reduces store visibility, and the
   3→4 star band is associated with roughly an 89% conversion difference.
3. **It also damages organic rank**, because both stores now weight post-install
   behaviour (`aso.md` §1). Badly-targeted paid UA is now *actively rank-negative.*

**£260 bought 500 installs and permanently degraded the free channel.** The paid spend was
recoverable; the rating was not, without a version reset.

The same source's derived rules are sound and worth adopting wholesale:
- **Ad promise must match the first-session experience**, or you are buying one-star reviews
- **Stop all ads immediately if the App Store rating drops ≥0.2 points in a week** — a kill
  switch that belongs in our runbook
- Their pre-spend gates: **D1 retention ≥30%, rating ≥4.0, ASO complete** — adopted into
  `triggers.md` §2

### The same source's genuinely useful positive data

From their creative test at **¥30,000/month (¥1,000/day)** on Meta **[ANECDOTAL]**:

| Creative | CPI | CTR |
|---|---|---|
| **A — feature-focused** | ¥250 | 1.2% |
| **B — emotion-focused** | **¥180** | **2.1%** |

**Emotion beat features on both CTR and CPI — a 28% CPI improvement from a creative angle
change alone.** Consistent with Matt Corey's Reddit winner ("End the bill panic"), which is
also a problem-state hook rather than a feature list. **Two independent indie datapoints
pointing the same way is the strongest creative guidance in this document.**

Their worked LTV example is also worth keeping as a template: ¥480/month subscription ×
5% conversion ÷ 10% monthly churn = **ARPU ¥24, LTV ¥240, CPI ceiling ¥120 (50% of LTV)**.

## 10. Hypercasual gaming — an entire business model where CPI overtook eCPM

**The category-level failure. Thousands of studios, one arithmetic problem.**

The hypercasual model was: buy installs cheaply, monetise with interstitial ads, keep the
spread. It worked while **CPI < eCPM**. Then it didn't.

- Post-ATT, **hypercasual CPI scaled faster than eCPM**, and "pure ad-driven economics
  stopped clearing as acquisition costs rose and ad demand softened on low-retention
  inventory"
  ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/the-state-of-the-hypercasual-genre-in-2024/),
  [Tenjin](https://tenjin.com/blog/from-hyper-to-hybrid-in-2024-ad-spend-cpi-by-platform-ad-network-country-rankings/)) **[DOCUMENTED]**
- **Hypercasual iOS ad spend fell 34% year-over-year** **[DOCUMENTED via Tenjin]**
- Downloads stayed high; eCPM fell; development and marketing costs both rose
- By 2023 the industry was openly declaring "hyper-casual is dead"
  ([Azur Games](https://azurgames.com/blog/forecast-for-the-hyper-sasual-market-in-the-second-half-of-2023/))

### Where are they now

**Pivoted, not dead.** The survivors became "hybrid-casual" — hypercasual-simple mechanics
with deeper progression, monetising **IAP + ads** rather than ads alone. Voodoo moved to
puzzle; SayGames and Supercent to idle-arcade
([Unity](https://unity.com/blog/mobile-gaming-shift-hyper-hybrid-casual)). **[DOCUMENTED]**
The studios that could not add retention-and-IAP depth did not survive.

### Diagnosis

Two structural lessons, both directly relevant to a portfolio studio:

1. **A business built on an arbitrage between two market prices you don't control will
   eventually be closed by the market.** Hypercasual studios weren't outcompeted — the
   spread between CPI and eCPM inverted, and every studio in the category went underwater
   simultaneously. **If our margin depends on a gap between two externally-set prices,
   we're renting a business, not building one.**
2. **The fix was retention, and it was the fix for everyone.** The industry's escape route
   from a *marketing* problem was a *product* change: deeper mechanics, IAP, better
   retention. That is the same conclusion as Quibi, Lensa, IRL, and the meditation app.

# PART THREE — THE PATTERN

Ten cases. Every single one resolves to the same finding.

| # | Case | Bootstrapped? | Outcome | Root cause |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cal AI | ✅ | Acquired (MyFitnessPal, Dec 2025) | Creators validated creative *before* paid scaled it |
| 2 | Umax / RizzGPT | ✅ | $15.4M ARR (Oct 2024); current status unknown | Product output *was* the ad |
| 3 | Matt Corey | ✅ | Partial — 5× cheaper CPA, still above ARPU | Monetisation was the binding constraint |
| 4 | Yuka | ✅ | ~80M users, profitable, **zero paid** | Sharing loop was a core interaction |
| 5 | Reflectly | ❌ ($5.97M) | Operating; 70% organic | Paid amplified an organic base |
| 6 | Quibi | ❌ ($1.75B) | Dead in 6 months | Bought installs into zero retention |
| 7 | Lensa AI | ❌ | 98% revenue decline in 17 months | One-shot novelty, no repeat use |
| 8 | IRL | ❌ ($170M) | Shut down; founder charged | Incentivised installs = fake growth |
| 9 | Meditation app | ✅ | £260 spent, rating 4.5→3.2 | Ad promise ≠ product experience |
| 10 | Hypercasual | mixed | Model collapsed; survivors pivoted | Margin depended on a spread they didn't control |

**Five findings, in order of how much they should change our behaviour:**

1. **Every failure is a retention or expectation-match failure wearing a marketing
   costume.** Not one of these five failures was caused by bad media buying. Quibi's media
   buying was excellent.

2. **Every success ran majority-organic, and paid amplified proven creative.** Cal AI,
   Umax, Reflectly — creators and organic first, then paid on what already worked. Yuka
   never entered the auction at all. **There is no case here of paid acquisition
   *discovering* a growth channel from a standing start.**

3. **A cheap install is not a good install.** The meditation app's £0.52 CPI was the most
   destructive number in this document. IRL's installs passed attribution and were
   worthless. **CPI is the metric most likely to make you feel successful while losing.**

4. **Emotion beats features in creative, on two independent indie datapoints.** ¥180 vs
   ¥250 CPI and 2.1% vs 1.2% CTR; "End the bill panic" over feature lists. Both are
   problem-state hooks. This is the cheapest available performance improvement we have
   evidence for.

5. **Measure with RPM vs CPM, not CPI or LTV:CAC.** Cal AI's frame is the only one that
   survives contact with post-ATT attribution at bootstrapped scale. It requires no MMP,
   no SKAdNetwork conversion values, and no assumptions about customer lifetime — just
   revenue and views, both of which we can count.
