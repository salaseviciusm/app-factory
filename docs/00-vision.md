# Vision

## What we're building

An **autonomous app company**: a system that takes an app idea expressed in conversation and
carries it through refinement, design, implementation, testing, launch, and ongoing marketing —
with a human founder guiding direction rather than doing the work.

The business model is a **revenue portfolio**: many small, monetized, genuinely useful mobile
apps. Winners get more investment; losers get killed or rebranded. Marketing automation is a
first-class part of the company, not an afterthought.

## What makes it a *company*, not a script

- **An org, not a pipeline.** Work is done by agents with defined roles and profiles
  (product, engineering leads, data engineering, QA, marketing) that collaborate, hand off
  work, and escalate — mirroring how a strong small team operates. See `02-agent-org.md`.
- **A daily rhythm.** A standup each morning where the founder reviews yesterday, guides
  priorities, and the chief-of-staff agent delegates the day's tasks across the org.
- **Institutional memory.** Principles, decisions, retro learnings, and post-launch data feed
  back into the docs and templates. The company gets better at making apps with every app.
- **Brand identity per project.** Each app is its own brand: name, visual identity, voice,
  positioning, marketing strategy. The company can spin up a new brand — or rebuild an
  existing app under a new brand — from a brand pack. See `04-template-brand-system.md`.

## What "good" looks like

- An idea can go from a chat message to a reviewed, testable build without the founder
  writing code.
- Every shipped app would pass an honest "would a human team be proud of this?" review:
  thought-through UX, tested, performant, differentiated.
- The founder's daily involvement is ~30 minutes: standup + approvals.
- Marketing runs continuously per app (content, ASO, scheduled posting, analytics-driven
  reallocation) with weekly founder review of the calendar.
- A rebrand (same core app, new identity + strategy) is a days-long operation, not a rewrite.

## What we deliberately do NOT automate

- **Store submission** — founder approves each submission after seeing screenshots and an
  installable build. Store credentials are never in fully-autonomous hands.
- **Outreach beyond our own channels** — automated posting happens on accounts we own, at
  human-approved cadence. Cold mass outreach is out: it burns accounts, domains, and brand.
- **Kill/pivot decisions** — agents recommend with data; the founder decides.

These gates exist for risk reasons (prompt injection, account bans, store policy) and because
they're cheap: minutes of human time guarding against company-ending failure modes.

## North-star constraints

- **Apple Guideline 4.3 / Google repetitive-content policy** shape throughput: 2–4 quality
  releases per month, each app genuinely differentiated — never a flood of near-clones.
- **Reuse without sameness**: shared code and principles internally, distinct brands externally.
